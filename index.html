AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    LSEG pays $3.5bn for credit data provider Octus

    LSEG agreed on 22 October to buy credit-intelligence provider Octus from Permira-advised funds for $3.535 billion in cash, its largest deal since Refinitiv, with Octus expected to pass $300 million of revenue in 2026 at an adjusted EBITDA margin of around 40% and closing due in the first half of 2027. LSEG · 2026-10-22

    A data vendor paying more than ten times revenue for proprietary credit data it says AI tools would struggle to replicate puts a market price on curated, licensable data, which is the argument a platform PM needs when making the case that a governed dataset is an asset rather than a cost line.

  2. 02
    FCA sets out good and poor practice for AI use

    On 19 October the FCA published examples of good and poor practice in firms' use of AI, drawn from supervisory work and its AI Lab and grouped under consumer outcomes, accountability and governance across the AI lifecycle, and operational resilience, with pre-deployment challenge panels and escalation routes tied to risk thresholds among the good examples and no new requirements added. Clifford Chance, on the FCA publication · 2026-10-19

    With the supervisor naming what good governance looks like instead of writing an AI rulebook, a UK financial-services platform team can map its lineage, pre-deployment review and escalation controls against these examples and use the gaps to rank the governance work on its roadmap.

  3. 03
    CustomerLake moves customer identity work into the lakehouse

    Databricks moved CustomerLake Campaign Agents to beta and Profile Agents to private preview on 19 October, governed by Unity Catalog and reading customer data in place through Lakehouse Federation, with HP projecting a possible 40% cut in CDP total cost of ownership and no general availability date or pricing announced. CMSWire · 2026-10-19

    For a Databricks estate, buy versus build on a customer data platform now includes running identity resolution inside the governed lakehouse instead of copying customer records into a separate marketing tool, though published results still show batch rather than real-time activation.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Lineage must now record why an automated decision was made

    Kroll argues that agentic AI adds a requirement on top of tracing data from source to use: decision lineage, a record of why an agent acted, which data it weighted most and which alternatives it rejected, which becomes harder to keep once data passes between agents in a multi-agent system. Kroll · 2026-10-14

    A gold layer with full source-to-table lineage still cannot explain an agent's answer, so the readiness plan needs a record of what each agent read and decided alongside the catalog before conversational access moves out of validation.

  2. 02
    43% of major AI initiatives expected to fail, HCLTech finds

    HCLTech's survey of 467 senior executives at companies with more than $1 billion in revenue found nearly 43% of major AI initiatives expected to fail, and put most of the risk on people and leadership readiness rather than tools, with AI deployed into workflows before the people working alongside it are prepared. HCLTech via AAP · 2026-05-20

    If the gap sits with people more than tools, onboarding time and governed coverage need a companion measure of whether consuming teams can actually use the governed data, which supports judging a foundation-phase platform on readiness rather than on adoption alone.

video · PlatformCon 2026, LinkedIn
The LinkedIn Entity Platform story: from siloed to streamlined

LinkedIn's data about members, companies and jobs is tightly connected, but for years each piece sat behind its own separately built service. Anyone who needed to combine them had to negotiate with several teams and stitch the answers together themselves. An entity platform puts those things behind one shared model and one checked way of asking for them, so a new consumer can query across them without a round of meetings. This fifteen-minute talk walks through that move from silos to one platform.

For the roadmap It is a worked example for the joins problem in onboarding: a shared entity model owned centrally, with domains publishing into it, is one answer to where modelling authority sits and how to make multi-source datasets onboard as quickly as simple ones.
