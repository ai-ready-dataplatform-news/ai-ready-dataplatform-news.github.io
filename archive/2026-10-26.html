AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Databricks gives agents a memory that learns from use

    Databricks put Agent Memory into beta on Agent Bricks on 20 October, built on its MemCurrent research and stored in Lakebase, splitting episodic memory of raw interactions from derived memory of patterns, preferences and workflows; on its own benchmark of 114 enterprise tasks, completion rose from 22.3% to 68.4% with 31% fewer tokens. Databricks blog · 2026-10-20

    Derived memory is a new store of business knowledge that sits outside the gold layer's agreed definitions, so keeping an agent's answer in line with the dashboard now means governing what agents remember as well as what they read.

  2. 02
    S&P Global opens private-markets data to outside AI tools

    S&P Global said on 22 October that iLEVEL, its portfolio monitoring platform used by more than 700 private markets firms, gains a native agent and AI-driven data ingestion, plus an MCP server that lets customers reach their iLEVEL data from tools such as Copilot and Claude with existing permissions and access controls kept in place. S&P Global via PR Newswire · 2026-10-22

    A data vendor shipping its own MCP server makes the licence, not the warehouse, the point where agent access is governed, which changes the buy-versus-build case for piping vendor data into the platform before anyone can use it.

  3. 03
    Salesforce agrees to buy Braze for $5.66 billion

    Salesforce agreed on 20 October to buy customer engagement platform Braze for about $5.66 billion, $47.50 a share in cash and roughly a 58% premium, planning to fold its real-time, cross-channel messaging into Data 360, Customer 360 and Agentforce, with closing expected in Salesforce's fiscal 2028. The Star (Reuters) · 2026-10-20

    Another application vendor pulling real-time customer data into its own data layer means more behavioural data generated and kept outside the enterprise platform, which raises the cost of keeping one governed customer record.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Data problems delayed or derailed AI at 86% of firms

    Iron Mountain's Information Supercycle report, surveyed by Vanson Bourne and released on 19 October, found data issues held up or derailed AI projects at 86% of organisations in the past year, 81% believe AI cannot scale past pilots until siloed and unstructured content is made AI-ready, and four in ten spend at least 41% of AI project time on manual data preparation. CDO Magazine · 2026-10-20

    Time spent on preparation is a number a foundation-phase platform can measure and drive down, and it makes a better early scorecard line than adoption: onboarding a governed dataset in under a week is the same claim in platform terms.

  2. 02
    Agents are in production before the data is trusted

    Interim results from The Modern Data Company's third Modern Data Survey, with more than 540 data leaders in 66 countries, show 57.3% piloting or running agents in data and analytics work but only 8.4% saying the data feeding them is trustworthy enough for production; even among teams already in production, 21.7% are very confident. IT Brief UK · 2026-08-19

    Data quality and trust outrank missing context and lineage as the barrier, 75.9% to 63.5%, which supports holding the governance bar on a narrow gold layer rather than widening scope to put agents on more data sooner.

  3. 03
    Nasuni lets agents read file shares under existing permissions

    Nasuni made AI Activate generally available on 6 October, connecting AI applications and agents to enterprise file data over MCP and enforcing existing file permissions before any content is returned, with permission-aware enterprise search to follow later this quarter. Pulse 2.0 · 2026-10-07

    Documents reached through the file layer bypass the platform's catalog and classification, so the readiness plan has to decide whether unstructured content is governed in the platform or left to permissions set by whoever owns the share.

The opportunity solution tree

Teams often jump from a goal straight to a list of features, and lose sight of why each one was chosen. An opportunity solution tree puts the goal at the top, then the customer needs and pain points that could move it, then the ideas that might meet each need, and at the bottom the small tests that show whether an idea works. Drawing it makes the team agree on which need it is working on before arguing about solutions, and shows which branches have never been tested.

For the roadmap For a platform roadmap the outcome is something like time to a trusted answer, the opportunities are the consumers' blockers such as joins, definitions and access waits, and the tree turns a backlog of platform features into bets each tied to a need someone actually has.
