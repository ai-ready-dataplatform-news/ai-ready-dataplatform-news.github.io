Data Contracts: the rise of modern data management

A data contract is a written agreement between whoever produces a dataset and whoever consumes it: what fields exist, what they mean, what changes are allowed and how consumers get warned before a breaking one ships. Without it, a producer renames a column or changes its meaning and every downstream dashboard, model or agent quietly breaks or, worse, keeps running on wrong numbers. Chad Sanderson's talk walks through why this became unavoidable once data started feeding models as well as dashboards: a human analyst might notice a weird number, a model usually won't.

For the roadmap A contract is the mechanism that lets domains own their data without the platform team re-checking every field by hand — directly the question of where modelling authority sits, and what an operating model needs a named owner for on either side of that boundary.
