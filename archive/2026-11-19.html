AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Databricks publishes what its customers actually spend on models

    Databricks launched the Databricks AI Index on 16 November, tracking token and spend shares across proprietary and open-weight models on its Unity Gateway; as reported from the index, open-weight models took 30.9% of tokens sent to Databricks-hosted models in the week of 2 November, up from 7.3% in the week of 20 July, and the median user's weekly spend rose from $0.40 to $2.49 over the same period. Databricks · 2026-11-16

    A cost-per-governed-dataset case that ignores model spend is now missing its fastest-growing line, and a sixfold rise in median per-user spend in four months gives a platform PM a dated benchmark to set an AI budget and gateway quotas against rather than a guess.

  2. 02
    Publishers' AI licensing collective signs Yahoo as first payer

    The Internet Licensing Collective, formerly the RSL Collective, launched on 12 November with more than 3,000 publisher members and Yahoo Scout as its first commercial licensee, paying a blanket fee for access and distributing royalties according to how each publisher's content appears in Yahoo's AI answers; the fee was not disclosed. Digiday · 2026-11-12

    It is a working template for pricing data sold to AI rather than to people: one blanket licence with payouts metered by use, which is the packaging question any team turning governed data into an external product has to answer before it signs a first buyer.

  3. 03
    Ex-Kusto engineers raise $70M for an agent-scale query engine

    Edelbyte, founded in 2025 by former Microsoft engineers who built Kusto, came out of stealth on 11 November with $70 million in seed and Series A funding led by Sequoia and Quiet Capital, for one engine combining search, analytics, vector, graph and geospatial queries, built for the query volume AI agents generate; it reports petabyte-scale deployments at several Fortune 500 companies, which is the company's own claim. The Bridge · 2026-11-11

    Investors are now betting that agent query load will break the warehouse cost model, so a platform PM opening governed data to agents should measure how many queries each conversational answer actually fires before that load lands on the compute bill.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Governance gaps stalled AI at 81% of large enterprises

    Transcend's survey of more than 220 senior IT leaders at enterprises with over 5,000 employees found 81% had at least one AI initiative delayed, scaled back or abandoned, with roughly a third of initiatives affected, and that data issues during the AI life cycle related to permissions or governance 93% of the time; Transcend, which sells governance software, says only a quarter have a governance model that runs in real time across every system. IT Brew, on Transcend's CIO data readiness report · 2026-06-10

    It places the blocker on the permissions layer rather than on data quality or models, which supports keeping the governance bar absolute inside a narrow scope and treating the access model, not more datasets, as the thing that has to scale before agents do.

  2. 02
    Databricks counts document-parsing AI spend in gateway budgets

    From 1 November, Unity Gateway budgets include spend from task-specific AI Functions such as ai_parse_document, ai_extract and ai_classify, across both batch and real-time execution paths. Databricks Unity Gateway release notes · 2026-11-01

    Turning documents into governed tables is now metered under the same budget as agent traffic, so the cost of making unstructured data model-ready can be attributed per pipeline and per team rather than vanishing into general compute.

  3. 03
    BigQuery's document parser reaches general availability

    Google made BigQuery's AI.PARSE_DOCUMENT function generally available on 2 November, extracting titles, headings, paragraphs, lists, tables and images from documents through the Document AI layout parser, which runs on a processor the customer creates and pays for separately. Data Studios, citing the BigQuery release notes · 2026-11-02

    With both Databricks and BigQuery now offering document parsing as a supported SQL function, the choice between bringing documents into the governed platform and running a separate pipeline is a cost-attribution decision, not a capability one.

Going all-in on data products

Most teams build an app first and let someone else work out the data afterwards, usually by scraping whatever the app happened to store. Auto Trader's team did it the other way round on a new online car-buying project: the same people who built the customer-facing product also designed the data that comes out of it, as a product in its own right. That data then fed a dashboard showing what the project delivered and even the billing system. It cost more effort up front, and the talk is open about where those costs landed.

For the roadmap It is a named case for putting data modelling authority with the team that builds the operational product, with the costs stated, which is the evidence a platform PM needs when arguing where modelling should sit; on a roadmap that means making a data product definition part of the definition of done for new operational features, rather than a platform backlog item afterwards.
