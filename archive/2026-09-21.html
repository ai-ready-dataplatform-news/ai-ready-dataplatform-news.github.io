The Product Operating Model

Most companies that say they've 'gone agile' are still running what Cagan calls a feature-team model: business stakeholders write requirements, and engineering builds them to spec on a dated roadmap. The product operating model is a different way of working — small, durable teams of a product manager, designer and engineers are handed a problem to solve, plus direct access to customers and data, rather than a spec to build. Success is judged on whether the problem actually got solved, not whether the ticket shipped on time. The change is organisational as much as anything: who holds the roadmap, who talks to customers, and who is accountable for the outcome all move.

For the roadmap The platform's own operating-model question — whether data-modelling authority sits centrally or with consuming domains — is the data-platform version of exactly this shift; reading it from the product side rather than the data side is what makes the argument legible outside data specifically.
