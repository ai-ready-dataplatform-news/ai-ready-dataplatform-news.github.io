AI news for Enterprise Data Platform Product Management

  1. 01
    Survey: enterprises can't track what AI compute costs

    A July-wave survey of 170 enterprises, published August 12, found fewer than half can rigorously track AI compute cost, most GPUs run at half capacity or less, and cost has slipped behind performance and availability as a buying criterion. VentureBeat

    If FinOps for AI workloads isn't yet a named line item on your platform roadmap, this is the data point to bring to the budget conversation.

  2. 02
    LiteLLM supply-chain breach hit 2,500+ companies' pipelines

    Researchers disclosed on August 11 that a compromised LiteLLM dependency exposed roughly 434,000 CI/CD pipelines across more than 2,500 organizations, with the FBI warning harvested credentials are still being weaponized months later. Cryptonomist

    Any AI gateway or LLM-routing library in your pipeline is now a supply-chain attack surface — worth an inventory check against this specific dependency, not just a general vendor-risk review.

  3. 03
    Snowflake makes video a first-class Cortex data type

    AI_MULTI_EMBED reached general availability on August 10 for semantic video search using TwelveLabs' Marengo Embed 3.0 model, indexing files up to 6GB directly inside Snowflake. Snowflake Documentation

    Video no longer has to sit outside the platform as a special case — worth revisiting any roadmap line that still treats unstructured video as someone else's pipeline.

  4. 04
    Iceberg community opens formal scoping for v4

    The Iceberg community opened a formal v4 scoping discussion this week covering manifest-format and column-update changes, with an explicit push to stay compatible with the row-aligned representation Delta Lake converged on in parallel. Apache Data Lakehouse Weekly

    Early-stage, but worth tracking if your lakehouse standardization decision assumed Iceberg v3 was the stable target for the next few years.

  5. 05
    OpenAI's Nextslide deal exposes a vendor-risk blind spot

    Nextslide's founder posted a farewell note on August 8 revealing he'd quietly joined OpenAI back in March — five months before the acquihire went public — with the app's data-deletion window open for just seven days after the original deal. THE D*AI*LY BRIEF

    Any AI vendor in your stack can go dark on similarly short notice — worth checking your own contracts for a disclosed-acquihire clause and a deletion window longer than a week.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Cloudera: legacy architecture is now the AI blocker

    Cloudera's global survey of 1,500 enterprise architects and data leaders, published August 11, found 95% have delayed AI projects over infrastructure limits, 73% say AI has made governance more complex, and 55% delayed or cancelled six or more AI projects in the past year over governance and compliance. Cloudera / GlobeNewswire

    If your roadmap still treats governance complexity as a downstream concern, this is the data showing it's already the top reason AI projects stall before they ship.

  2. 02
    SAP and Google Cloud connect BDC to BigQuery natively

    SAP Business Data Cloud Connect for BigQuery reached general availability on August 12, giving zero-copy access to SAP data products directly from BigQuery without a separate extraction pipeline. Google Cloud Blog

    One less bespoke pipeline between SAP's operational data and whatever's consuming it downstream — worth checking against any SAP-to-warehouse ETL still on the books.

Five real blueprints for modernizing without breaking

When a company swaps out its core data platform — say, moving a warehouse from one vendor to another — the risk isn't the migration itself, it's breaking the dashboards and pipelines everyone depends on while it happens. A blueprint approach means borrowing a pattern that already worked elsewhere: decentralizing ownership so one team's change doesn't ripple into everyone else's, or pairing domain-owned data with shared infrastructure so teams keep autonomy without losing consistency. The useful part isn't which tools got swapped — it's the sequencing: what kept running in parallel, and what the actual cutover trigger was.

For the roadmap Before greenlighting your own platform migration, borrow the sequencing question these blueprints answer — what runs in parallel and what triggers the cutover — rather than just the target-architecture diagram.
