AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

course · Regulation AI Academy
AI Act Fundamentals: a free five-module course

The EU AI Act doesn't regulate AI in one lump; it sorts systems into risk tiers — unacceptable, high-risk, limited-risk, minimal-risk — and attaches different duties to each. A customer-facing chatbot mainly owes people a disclosure that they're talking to AI; a system used in credit decisions or fraud detection sits in the high-risk tier and owes a risk-management file, human oversight and logging that a regulator can inspect. The course walks through those tiers, the deadlines attached to each, and who has to prove what.

For the roadmap DORA already forces documented ICT risk management for anything a bank's platform touches; the AI Act's Article 9(10) lets that same risk file double up for AI obligations rather than building a parallel one — which makes 'which of our governed datasets feed a high-risk system' a mapping exercise worth doing on this platform now, ahead of the tightened 2027 deadline rather than after it.
