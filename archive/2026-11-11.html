AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Hong Kong's bank regulator sets out how agents should be governed

    On 2 November the HKMA launched Project SAGE (Securing Agentic Finance with Governance and Enablement) with an Agentic Finance Expert Group, and published a discussion paper on agentic AI governance in banking that asks for industry views on risk-based, proportionate controls scaled to a use case's materiality and the agent's degree of autonomy. Hong Kong Monetary Authority · 2026-11-02

    A banking regulator is now describing agent controls in specific terms (a registry of material agents, accountability tied to an agent's intended purpose, intervention mechanisms), so a financial-services platform team can treat agent identity and an inventory of agents as roadmap items to plan for now rather than wait for UK rules.

  2. 02
    Anthropic reportedly licenses AP election data for Claude

    Adweek reported on 4 November, citing two people familiar with the matter, that Anthropic struck a content and data licensing deal with the Associated Press ahead of the US midterms, under which Claude showed election-night results attributed to AP; terms were not disclosed and neither company has commented on the record. Adweek · 2026-11-04

    If confirmed, it would be the first time Anthropic has paid a publisher for live, attributed data rather than training text, so a data vendor pricing a feed now has to decide whether model providers are a separate licence tier with their own attribution and freshness terms.

  3. 03
    Tableau Data Apps enter beta with inherited permissions

    Tableau described Data Apps as in beta on 3 November: apps built in Tableau Studio, or by any third-party AI agent, publish to Tableau Cloud, Server or Tableau Next and inherit Tableau's existing data access controls and permissions, with general availability for Tableau Server on its roadmap for November 2026. Tableau blog · 2026-11-03

    When apps written by outside AI agents publish straight into the BI layer, the BI tool's permission model becomes the control point, so a platform team should decide whether those apps read governed gold-layer tables through the same policies as dashboards or end up bypassing them.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Genie learns metric definitions from dbt runs and Power BI

    From 2 November, Genie Ontology (in public preview) extracts snippets from queries that external tools such as dbt run on Databricks, and a beta external knowledge sources feature turns the measures, calculated columns and relationships in Power BI semantic models into the same Ontology snippets. Databricks documentation · 2026-11-02

    Letting the agent learn its definitions from the transformation layer and the BI models already in use is a direct test of whether the agent's answer can match the dashboard's; the open governance question is who sees those snippets, since one secondary write-up says they reach every user in the account regardless of their Power BI access.

podcast · Experiencing Data (Brian T. O'Neill)
Buyers Block: Why Your B2B Analytics or AI Product's POC Didn't Close

A trial run of a data or AI product can go well, with the buyer saying nice things, and the sale still quietly dies. That happens because liking a demo is not the same as being able to see what the product changes in their own week. O'Neill calls this buyer's block, and his argument is that more sales effort rarely fixes it: the product has to make its value obvious, ask little effort of the customer, and keep showing that value after the trial ends.

For the roadmap The same test applies to an internal platform in its foundation phase: a successful pilot with a consuming team is not adoption, so the scorecard should check whether the value stays visible to that team after the pilot, which is a better early signal than a usage count.
