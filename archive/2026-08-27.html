AI news for Enterprise Data Platform Product Management

  1. 01
    South Korea opens AI training to non-consented data

    South Korea's National Assembly passed a Personal Information Protection Act amendment on 20 August letting firms use original, non-pseudonymized personal data for AI development without consent, subject to Personal Information Protection Commission approval and a risk assessment. IAPP · 2026-08-20

    A regulator-approval pathway for raw personal data is now a line item on the data-sourcing risk checklist for any platform training on APAC user data.

  2. 02
    Google: security tops the list of AI-scaling blockers

    Google Cloud's State of AI Infrastructure report, surveying more than 1,400 senior IT leaders, found 79% cite security, governance or operations as their biggest obstacle to scaling AI inference. Google Cloud Blog · 2026-08-24

    Governance readiness, not model capability, is what respondents say actually gates the next wave of agent rollouts — reason to fund provenance and access-control work ahead of the next push, not after an incident.

  3. 03
    Databricks drops Terraform as its deployment default

    Databricks made its Direct Deployment Engine the default for Asset Bundles from 26 August, removing the Terraform dependency and promising roughly 40% faster deployments ahead of the Terraform engine's retirement in September. SunnyData, on Databricks · 2026-08-26

    Any pipeline still wired to Bundles-on-Terraform needs a migration plan before September — worth a line in the next platform-engineering sprint rather than a scramble after it's switched off.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    43% call legacy APIs the agentic AI blocker

    The same Google Cloud survey found 43% of IT leaders name difficulty integrating legacy APIs and data sources as their biggest agentic-AI infrastructure gap, and points to its Agentic Data Cloud — BigQuery and Spanner reachable over open Iceberg and Spark standards — as the intended fix. Google Cloud Blog · 2026-08-24

    The real question for the next platform bake-off isn't model access, it's whether legacy systems can be exposed through open standards without a bespoke integration project.

A warehouse stores tables for fast queries; a lake stores raw files cheaply but without much structure; a lakehouse tries to get both at once, using an open table format like Iceberg to make a folder of files behave like a table. The course has you build small versions of each with real tools, so the difference between the three stops being a diagram and becomes something you've actually assembled by hand.

For the roadmap Most 'is our data AI-ready' conversations are really asking whether the platform underneath is a lake, a warehouse, or a lakehouse — worth having a first-principles answer to that before the next platform debate happens in a room full of half-remembered vendor decks.
