AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Microsoft retires Purview Data Governance for the OneLake catalog

    Microsoft has given notice that support for Purview Data Governance, including the Unified Catalog and Data Map, ends on 12 November 2027, with governance of Fabric data moving to the OneLake catalog while Purview stays on as the security and compliance layer; Microsoft names Atlan, Alation, Collibra and Informatica as integrations for estates that reach beyond Fabric. Microsoft Tech Community, Analytics on Azure blog · 2026-11-13

    Anyone weighing catalog buy-versus-build on a Microsoft-heavy estate now has a twelve-month clock and a fork to choose: govern inside the platform's own catalog, or keep an independent governance layer that spans Databricks, Redshift and Power BI alike.

  2. 02
    Redshift commits several writes to one Iceberg table atomically

    From Patch 206, Amazon Redshift runs several INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE or MERGE statements against one Apache Iceberg table inside a single BEGIN … COMMIT and publishes them as one snapshot, so other sessions and engines see none of the changes until commit; all writes in a transaction must target the same table, and AWS asks Glue Data Catalog users to switch on orphan-file deletion first. AWS Big Data Blog · 2026-11-13

    A warehouse that can now do a governed, all-or-nothing load into an open table changes the cost of keeping Redshift alongside a lakehouse: gold-layer refreshes no longer have to be single statements to stay consistent for the dashboards and agents reading them.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Snowflake makes scored, optimised AI functions generally available

    Snowflake made Cortex AI Function Studio and Cortex AI Function Optimization generally available on 16 November, letting a team build a custom AI function in SQL, score it against a labelled, versioned sample of about 50 to 200 rows, and have Snowflake search for a better prompt or a cheaper model before it reaches production; no separate pricing is published for either. Data Studios, citing the Snowflake release notes · 2026-11-16

    It turns 'is the data good enough for the model' into a measured gate inside the warehouse, with a labelled sample as the contract, which is the pattern a governed gold layer needs before any AI-derived column is allowed to feed a dashboard.

  2. 02
    Atlan offers a route out of Purview that keeps data products

    Atlan announced on 17 November a collaboration with Microsoft to extend governance across the OneLake catalog, including a Migration Studio, in private preview, that reads Purview Data Governance and carries glossaries, governance domains, data products, custom attributes and classifications across, and exposes Atlan's metadata in OneLake through Iceberg shortcuts in preview. Yahoo Finance, on Atlan's announcement · 2026-11-17

    Data products, domains and glossary terms are now being treated as portable metadata rather than something rebuilt per catalog, which is the test to set any catalog vendor: can the definitions agreed for a gold layer leave with you.

  3. 03
    A hospital took 18 months on its data before scaling AI

    Hospital for Special Surgery's chief transformation and digital information officer describes asking his board for 18 months to centralise the organisation's data before scaling AI, declining one-off use cases until the data was right, and ties that sequencing to a reported $7M return from healthcare AI, though the episode listing does not say how the figure was calculated. The Data & AI Chief (ThoughtSpot), episode 149, Dr. Ashis Barad of HSS · 2026-11-11

    It is a named, board-level case for judging a platform on its foundation before its adoption numbers, which is the argument a foundation-phase platform team has to win when asked why AI use cases are not yet multiplying.

AI in Financial Services: Foundations through Future Trends

Banks and insurers already use AI to spot fraud, score credit and answer customers, and each of those only works if the data underneath can be reached, trusted and shared. This three-course series starts with how such systems are built and where they go wrong, then turns to why data access and interoperability decide which AI ideas are even possible, and ends with open finance, where customers can tell their bank to share their data with someone else. The thread through all three is that in finance the rules about data shape the AI more than the models do.

For the roadmap It frames AI governance in financial services as a data-access and interoperability question rather than a model question, which gives a platform PM the vocabulary to put consent, sharing and interoperability requirements on the roadmap now, ahead of the AI use cases that will depend on them.
