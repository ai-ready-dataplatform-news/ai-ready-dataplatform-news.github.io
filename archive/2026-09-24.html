case study · Data Products (Chad Sanderson)
Tackling Data's Biggest Culture Problem

A data contract is just a promise: the team that owns a database tells everyone downstream exactly what shape the data will be in, and what happens if that shape changes, instead of consumers discovering the breakage in production. At Convoy, Chad Sanderson didn't start by writing contracts — he started by getting engineers and data people to talk to each other at all, because the real problem wasn't the schema, it was that the two groups had no shared way to even notice they depended on each other. The contracts came after the conversation existed, not before.

For the roadmap The lesson transfers directly to a governed-gold-layer roadmap: the harder problem is creating the standing conversation between the team that owns a source system and the team that consumes it, not writing the schema — worth treating as its own workstream rather than a byproduct of the tooling.
