AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Snowflake puts agents on pipeline repair, engineers approve

    At Expedition 2026 on 3 November Snowflake announced what it calls autonomous data engineering: CoCo Cloud Agents run its coding agent in a Snowflake-managed environment rather than on an engineer's laptop, and CoCo Automations run those agents on a schedule to detect and repair pipeline faults, alerting engineers when a fix is ready for them to approve into production. Snowflake · 2026-11-03

    Agents that edit pipelines move the control point from writing code to approving changes, so a platform PM weighing what the transformation framework should standardise needs review gates, lineage and contract tests that hold whether a person or an agent made the change.

  2. 02
    First Advantage buys the data supplier it already used

    Background-screening firm First Advantage said on 5 November it has acquired Data Diver Technologies, an AI and automation platform that collects, aggregates and delivers high-volume criminal records data and was already one of its third-party data providers, for about $125 million, framing the deal as strengthening its proprietary data. Citybiz, on First Advantage's 5 November announcement · 2026-11-05

    A buyer paying to own its data supply shows where the margin in a data product sits; for anyone pricing or running an external data feed, it is a reminder that a supplier whose data is core to a client's product is also an acquisition target, and that supply terms should cover that case.

  3. 03
    EDB buys Altinity to pair Postgres with ClickHouse

    EDB announced on 5 November that it has acquired Altinity, the second-largest independent contributor to ClickHouse, for undisclosed terms; Altinity becomes EDB's Real-Time Analytics group, ClickHouse and Altinity's builds stay Apache 2.0, and integrated enterprise ClickHouse capabilities are promised for Q4 2026. Blocks and Files · 2026-11-06

    One vendor now supports both the transactional and the real-time analytical engine, which changes the buy-versus-build sum for estates running Postgres operationally and wanting fast analytics without a warehouse hop; the open-licence commitment is the clause to check before betting a roadmap on it.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Snowflake gives agents and dashboards one set of definitions

    Snowflake's Business Ontology, announced at Expedition 2026 on 3 November inside Horizon Catalog, lets teams define and approve business concepts such as net revenue, assign each an owner and bind it to tables, views and Semantic Views, so people and agents use the same approved meaning; reports differ on whether it is in private or upcoming public preview. InfoWorld · 2026-11-03

    This is the agent-versus-dashboard consistency problem built into the catalog: the decision it sharpens is whether business definitions are owned once, centrally, with a named owner per term, rather than encoded separately in BI models and agent prompts that drift apart.

  2. 02
    Bank of England's data chief sets out three missions

    Ben Martin, the Bank of England's Chief Data Officer, told the DAMA Data and AI Conference Europe on 4 November that the Bank's data and analytics transformation runs on three missions, including making data easy for colleagues to work with and building solid foundations, and that it uses DAMA's data management framework as the basis for that work rather than as a standalone control. Bank of England · 2026-11-04

    A central bank treating its data management framework as the foundation of delivery, not a compliance checklist, is a reference point for a financial-services platform team arguing that capability maturity should be judged by what it enables, which bears directly on how a foundation-phase platform is measured.

At John Lewis Partnership, measuring developer platform value

A platform team builds the shared roads other teams drive on, so the hard question is how to show the roads are worth paying for. Counting how many teams use them proves little when using them is compulsory. John Lewis Partnership's platform team moved from measuring lead time, to the four DORA delivery measures, to a Technical Health score: small automated jobs that check each team against more than 35 measures, such as security and operational readiness, and show the result as traffic lights.

For the roadmap It is a worked example of the argument that adoption is the wrong headline metric early on. A data platform PM can borrow the pattern and swap in data measures, such as governed coverage, time to onboard a dataset and contract-test pass rate, so the scorecard shows whether the platform reduces friction rather than whether people were made to use it.
