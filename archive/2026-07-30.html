AI news for Enterprise Data Platform Product Management

  1. 01
    Databricks and Microsoft extend partnership into the 2030s

    Databricks and Microsoft expanded their decade-long partnership on July 23, with Databricks moving its own core operations onto Microsoft's newer Azure Cobalt 200 infrastructure and deepening Genie integration across Microsoft's products. Microsoft Source · 2026-07-23

    Signals Azure as an increasingly default runtime for Databricks workloads — worth weighing against any multi-cloud or cloud-neutrality commitments already on the platform roadmap.

  2. 02
    lakeFS ships AI governance features ahead of AI Act deadline

    lakeFS's Summer 2026 release, out July 29, adds automated governance and control over the unstructured and structured data feeding AI models and agents, timed to the EU AI Act's August 2 transparency obligations. BigDATAwire · 2026-07-29

    A concrete data-versioning option for the governance gap the AI Act creates this week — worth evaluating against building equivalent lineage controls in-house.

  3. 03
    MCP's enterprise authorization extension goes stable

    The Model Context Protocol's July 28 specification update promotes its Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension to stable, giving organizations a standard way to gate MCP server access through their identity provider, already adopted by Anthropic, Microsoft and Okta. InfoQ · 2026-07-28

    Removes a real blocker to putting MCP servers in front of production data — worth checking whether current identity-provider integration plans already account for this extension.

  4. 04
    Enterprise storage rebuilds itself around AI-ready data

    Fragmented, ungoverned data is stalling AI initiatives even where GPU spend is already in place, and storage spending rose 22.7% in Q1 2026 as companies prioritize infrastructure that makes data usable for AI. Forbes · 2026-07-24

    Reframes the next platform investment case: the bottleneck to fund isn't more compute, it's the data-readiness layer underneath it.

  5. 05
    Polaris pulls its 1.7.0 release over a licensing gap

    Apache Polaris found a licensing gap across 44 staged jars while trying to ship version 1.7.0 and pulled the release candidate, part of a broader push this week across Iceberg, Parquet and Arrow toward stricter, written spec guarantees. Apache Data Lakehouse Weekly · 2026-07-29

    A pulled release over licensing hygiene is a reason to audit dependency provenance before pinning a Polaris version into production catalog infrastructure.
