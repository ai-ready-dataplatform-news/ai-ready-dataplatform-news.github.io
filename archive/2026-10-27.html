AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    Oracle puts business definitions under its lakehouse's agents

    At AI World on 26 October Oracle announced a Business Semantic Layer for Autonomous AI Lakehouse, holding dimensions, measures, hierarchies and KPIs that feed Select AI and its other natural-language tools, alongside an AI Data Catalog reaching external catalogs, databases and Iceberg tables without copying data; both are promised within 12 months rather than available now. Oracle via PR Newswire · 2026-10-26

    Every major platform now argues that agents should read agreed definitions rather than raw tables, which makes where the semantic model lives, in the platform, the BI tool or a neutral layer, the decision that keeps an agent's answer matching the dashboard.

  2. 02
    86% of UK financial firms now use AI, regulators find

    The fourth joint Bank of England and FCA survey, published on 26 October, found 86% of responding firms using AI against 75% in 2024, generative AI making up 56% of reported systems, operations the most common use at 64%, and 30% of small firms naming data protection and IT systems as a constraint on deployment. Bank of England and FCA · 2026-10-26

    With the EU AI Act cited by 71% of firms and the FCA holding to existing rules rather than an AI rulebook, a UK financial-services platform's governance case rests on showing lineage and controls under rules it already answers to, not on waiting for new ones.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Semantic layers are built, but agents read documents instead

    VentureBeat's monthly VB Pulse survey found the share of enterprises running, piloting or building a governed semantic layer rose from 58% in June to 67% in August, while the share naming it their agents' main source of business context fell from 21% to 13%, behind document search at 32%. VentureBeat · 2026-10-06

    A gold layer with agreed definitions only keeps agentic and analytical answers aligned if agents are actually routed through it, so the readiness plan needs a measure of which context agents use, not just whether the semantic layer exists.

  2. 02
    BigQuery makes document chunking a governed SQL function

    Google's BigQuery release notes for 21 October list AI.CHUNK_DOCUMENT as generally available, a table-valued function that splits unstructured documents into text chunks for embeddings and retrieval-augmented generation inside the warehouse. BigQuery release notes · 2026-10-21

    Chunking inside the platform keeps documents under the same access rules and lineage as tables, which strengthens the case for governing unstructured content in the platform rather than in a separate retrieval pipeline nobody catalogues.

  3. 03
    Only 5% say their data is largely ready for AI

    Further's State of Enterprise AI Maturity report, a survey of 515 US AI decision-makers, found 5% believe their data is largely ready for AI, 37% paused, scaled back or abandoned an AI initiative in the past year with data quality or availability the top reason at 24%, and only 29% have completed a formal data audit and remediation plan. THE Journal · 2026-10-26

    A completed data audit with a remediation plan is something under a third of firms can show, which makes it a credible foundation-phase scorecard line alongside onboarding time while adoption is still too early to judge.

Data mesh in practice: product thinking and development

Most companies treat data as a by-product: one team makes it, another team cleans it up later, and nobody owns whether it is useful. Product thinking flips that. Each dataset someone depends on gets an owner, a known set of users, a promise about what it contains and how fresh it is, and the same care a team would give an app. This series follows how that was done at a large pharmaceutical company, starting from who would use each data product and working backwards to what it had to provide.

For the roadmap It gives a worked pattern for the central-versus-domain modelling question: define each data product by its consumers and its promises first, and the question of who owns the model becomes a question of who answers for those promises.
