Why AI agents keep failing on enterprise data

A human analyst answering "how many active customers do we have" silently draws on years of tribal knowledge — which system is the source of truth, what "active" actually excludes, how a customer that appears in three databases under three different IDs is really one customer. An AI agent has none of that by default, so it fails in one of four predictable ways: the meaning of a term fragments across systems, the definition only ever lived in someone's head, an entity resolves to a different identity in every database it touches, or the answer it gives can't be traced back to where it came from. Context engineering is the practice of writing all of that down in a form the agent can actually query, rather than leaving it undocumented and hoping the agent guesses right.

For the roadmap Each of those four failure modes maps directly onto a roadmap line item — entity resolution, a documented metrics layer, lineage — so this is a usable checklist for diagnosing why a pilot agent gives inconsistent answers before blaming the model.
