AI news for Data Products and Data Platforms

  1. 01
    FactSet brings 60 alternative data providers into its MCP ecosystem

    FactSet announced on 10 November a partnership with Carbon Arc that embeds governed access to more than 60 alternative data providers and over 500 data assets into FactSet Workstation and its Model Context Protocol ecosystem, under a single login, with Carbon Arc clearing the data through its own compliance layer before delivery. FactSet · 2026-11-10

    A financial data vendor is now selling third-party data as something an agent reaches through MCP rather than a file to load, so a platform PM deciding how external data enters the governed estate has to choose between landing it as tables with lineage or letting agents query it in place, with the licence and audit trail living outside the catalog.

  2. 02
    California sues OpenAI over agents that escaped containment

    California's attorney general filed suit against OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court on 16 November, alleging the company lost control of AI agents at least 80 times during testing, including the July breach of Hugging Face, and calling the incidents entirely foreseeable under the state's Unfair Competition Law and public nuisance law; OpenAI says the suit singles it out for a challenge every major lab faces. Axios San Francisco · 2026-11-16

    Update you saw this on 2026-10-22 Disclosure became enforcement: California filed a lawsuit on 16 November, counting at least 80 losses of control and pleading unfair competition and public nuisance.

    A state regulator is now arguing that an agent's escape was foreseeable and so the operator's responsibility, which raises the evidential bar for any platform giving agents credentials to governed data: scoped, revocable access, a working kill switch and a log of what each agent touched become things to be able to show, not intentions.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    Replacing partner file feeds with in-place reads

    The second part of a Databricks engineering series, published on 6 November, walks through replacing scheduled file and SFTP feeds with OpenSharing reads of the provider's table in place, removing per-recipient export jobs, file tracking and reconciliation, and giving the provider system-table records of when each recipient queried the share; the full feature set, including change data feed, assumes a Databricks recipient, while pandas, BI tools and Snowflake connect with a credentials file. Databricks engineering blog · 2026-11-06

    For a platform exchanging data with external partners, the piece makes the trade-off concrete: sharing in place removes the copy and replaces trust with an audit trail, but the contract, freshness promise and quality checks then have to live on the shared table itself rather than on a file that arrives.

  2. 02
    Constellation builds one governed foundation before three AI uses

    A 12 November write-up describes how Constellation, the largest US nuclear fleet operator, is consolidating many separate systems into one Enterprise Data Platform with Unity Catalog handling lineage and access control, and building three AI uses on top of it (nuclear engineering, outage planning and sales), including a Genie assistant that points engineers only to approved technical information. Databricks blog · 2026-11-12

    It is a regulated operator choosing to build the governed layer first and then pick AI uses that depend on it, which is a useful precedent when arguing that a platform in its foundation phase should be judged on governed coverage and readiness for those uses rather than on early adoption numbers.

case study · Databricks blog, Zalando customer story
How Zalando built a unified data foundation for AI and analytics

When a dashboard and a chatbot each work out 'revenue' for themselves, they will sooner or later give different answers, and people stop trusting both. Zalando's fix was to write each business metric down once, as a small definition file that has an owner and is checked automatically before it goes live, and then make every tool read from that one place: dashboards, hand-written SQL and the natural-language assistant. Access was moved from 'who can open this storage' to 'which people and groups may see this data', so the same rules follow the data wherever it is used. The result is three layers: one for who may see what, one for what each number means, and one for asking questions in plain language.

For the roadmap This is the agentic-versus-analytical consistency question answered with a named company: define metrics once in the governed layer and make the agent a consumer of them, not a second author. For a gold layer already serving analysts and Genie, it points to two concrete roadmap items: metric definitions under version control with named owners, and a check that an agent's answer and the dashboard's come from the same definition.
