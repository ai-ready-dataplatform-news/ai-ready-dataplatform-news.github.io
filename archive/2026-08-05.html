AI news for Enterprise Data Platform Product Management

  1. 01
    Only 6% of enterprises say their data is AI-ready

    Dun & Bradstreet's AI Momentum survey of 10,000 businesses found most now report some AI ROI, but only 6% say their enterprise data is "fully ready" to support AI at scale, with 47% only partially ready. Dun & Bradstreet · 2026-07-28

    ROI is catching up to adoption but the data-readiness bottleneck hasn't moved — a concrete number to cite when the next agent rollout needs a data-foundation budget line, not just a model budget.

  2. 02
    lakeFS ties its governance release to the EU AI Act deadline

    lakeFS's Summer 2026 Enterprise release adds versioned, immutable "datasets" spanning objects, Iceberg tables and namespaces, timed to the EU AI Act's August 2 enforcement date. Database Trends and Applications · 2026-07-29

    A vendor is now selling EU AI Act compliance as a data-versioning feature rather than a policy document — worth a look before building an equivalent audit trail in-house.

  3. 03
    92% of AI breaches happened with no access controls

    IBM's 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report found 92% of organizations with an AI-related security incident had no access controls on the AI system involved, and only 40% use access controls on AI models and data at all. IBM / Ponemon Institute · 2026-07-29

    Access control on models and training data is the cheapest lever in the report and the one most platforms still skip — worth a line item in the next governance review, before an incident forces it.

  4. 04
    DataBahn raises $40M for an agentic data control plane

    DataBahn closed a $40M Series B led by Insight Partners for infrastructure that prepares, governs and routes enterprise data between operational systems, security tools and AI models before it reaches them. SiliconANGLE · 2026-07-30

    Investors are betting the pre-processing layer in front of AI, not the model layer, is where the next platform category gets built — a build-vs-buy signal for any team assembling its own data-prep pipeline for agents.

  5. 05
    DigitalOcean raises GPU droplet prices on strong AI demand

    DigitalOcean raised on-demand and 12-month reserved pricing on select NVIDIA and AMD GPU droplets effective August 1, citing sustained demand for AI compute capacity. DigitalOcean · 2026-08-01

    When even commodity-cloud GPU capacity reprices mid-year, a TCO model built on last quarter's rate card needs a re-check before the next compute commitment.

AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

  1. 01
    97% of firms build data products, 25% do it repeatably

    A Quest Software-backed survey of data leaders found 97% of organizations deliver data products, but only 25% do so through a structured, repeatable program rather than ad hoc effort. Forbes / Quest Software · 2026-07-31

    The AI-ready gap for most platforms is process maturity, not tooling — worth auditing how many roadmap "data products" are actually repeatable before scaling them to agents.

  2. 02
    ThoughtSpot ships a semantic layer built for agents, not dashboards

    Spotter Semantics translates natural-language questions into deterministic SQL rather than raw LLM text-to-SQL, and aligns with the Open Semantic Interchange initiative to work against Snowflake and Databricks models. ThoughtSpot · 2026-03-12

    A deterministic-SQL path through the semantic layer is a concrete alternative to LLM-only text-to-SQL for teams worried about agent answers drifting — worth a bake-off before standardizing on one query path.

  3. 03
    Gartner's governance evaluation now covers AI outputs, not just tables

    The 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Data and Analytics Governance Platforms expanded its evaluation criteria to unstructured data, ML features, model versions and AI-generated outputs, not just structured tables. Ataccama, on the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant · 2026-01-09

    Governance tooling bought for BI-era catalogs may not cover what AI actually touches — worth checking the current governance vendor's roadmap against this wider scope, not its old feature list.

  4. 04
    81% feel ready to scale AI, 75% admit the operating model isn't

    Deloitte's 2026 Global Technology Leadership Study of 660+ executives found 81% say they can deploy and govern AI at scale today, yet nearly 75% admit their operating model must change in the next 12–18 months to sustain it. Deloitte · 2026-06-01

    Confidence in deployment capability is outpacing operating-model readiness — a case for pairing any new platform capability with an explicit owner and workflow change, not just a tool rollout.

  5. 05
    Data management capability still trails AI ambition, 435-org benchmark finds

    The EDM Association's 2026 Global Data Management Benchmark, surveying 435+ organizations across 50+ countries, found AI investment accelerating well ahead of the governance and data-management foundations needed to support it. EDM Council · 2026-05-19

    A peer-comparable benchmark number is useful ammunition when foundational data-management funding is competing against the next flashy AI-feature ask.

Is Your Data AI-Ready? The Semantic Layer and the Last Mile Problem

A semantic layer is the dictionary that sits between raw database tables and whoever is asking questions of them — it says "revenue" means this column, summed this way, with refunds excluded. BI tools built that dictionary assuming a human would read the final chart and notice if something looked off. An AI agent doesn't read a chart; it acts on the answer directly, so an ambiguous or stale entry in the dictionary doesn't just show a wrong number, it drives a wrong action. That gap between data being clean and data being usable by a machine is the "last mile problem": AI-ready data needs lineage, freshness, and a semantic layer written for a machine reader instead of a human one.

For the roadmap The episode's claim that most AI projects stall on data understanding rather than model quality reframes the roadmap: a machine-readable semantic layer belongs in the plan as its own funded workstream, not as a side effect of the next agent feature.
