AI-ready vs analytical-ready data

blog · MIT CISR · 2026-05-01
The Case for a Semantic Layer

A semantic layer is a shared dictionary that sits between raw data and everyone who uses it — a person building a dashboard, or an AI agent answering a question. It says 'this column means revenue, defined this way, in this currency' once, instead of every tool and every person guessing for themselves. Without it, two people — or two AI agents — asking about 'active customers' can get two different, equally confident answers, with no way to tell which one is right. MIT's researchers found that teams who build this dictionary well for human analysts usually end up with AI-ready data almost by accident, because the same clear definitions an analyst needs are exactly what stops an AI agent from picking the wrong table or the wrong calculation.

For the roadmap If agent pilots keep stalling on 'the numbers don't match', the fix belongs on the semantic-layer backlog, not the model-selection one — treat metric definitions as a governed product with an owner, not a side effect of the BI rollout.
